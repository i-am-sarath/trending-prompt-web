Where the blister-pack prompt sells the toy through its packaging, this one sells it through context: three objects that only make sense together. A figure, the 3D sculpt of that figure on a monitor, and the retail box with its illustration. Each one is a different rendering of the same person, and asking the model to keep all three consistent forces it to commit to a single, clear design.

The 1/7 scale callout and the clear acrylic base matter more than they look. Scale tells the model how big the figure is next to a monitor and keyboard, and the acrylic base is the single most recognisable detail of premium collectible figures. Drop it and you tend to get a figure standing on bare wood that reads as a small person rather than a toy.

Keep the base free of text and the box brand fictional. Both are where models are most likely to invent garbled lettering or reach for a real toy company's logo.
