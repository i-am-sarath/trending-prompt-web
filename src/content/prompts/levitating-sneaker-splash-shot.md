The most important sentence in this prompt is in the first section: "Do not invent or change any design elements." Product prompts are judged on fidelity, and without that explicit instruction models freely "improve" the stitching, the panels and the logo placement. That is fine for art, and fatal for an ad.

The splash is the second-best-known trick in product photography after the floating shot, and putting them together works because each helps the other. Water gives the levitation physical context and motion, and the sneaker gives the splash a clear centre. Keep the macro lens and high-speed flash language: it tells the model the water should be frozen and razor sharp, not blurred.

The floor shadow far below is a small but essential detail. Without it the sneaker looks pasted onto a gradient. With it, there is a real distance between the shoe and the ground, and the eye believes it is hovering. Swap water for powder, sand or petals to suit other products.
