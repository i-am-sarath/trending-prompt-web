This one only works if the model gets two references, so the header says so in capitals before anything else. Giving the model a single photo and asking it to "imagine my younger self" produces a generic child who shares nothing with you except hair colour; giving it an actual childhood picture turns the request into a compositing job, which current image models are far better at.

STYLING is the quiet load-bearing clause. Telling the model to keep each version in the clothes from its own reference is what makes the picture read as two moments in time rather than a parent and child dressed by the same stylist.

FILM CHARACTER does the rest. The square frame, thick bottom border and direct flash belong to a very specific kind of family snapshot, and that association carries most of the emotion. Leave the border blank: models still misspell handwriting, and an empty border looks more real than a wrong date.
