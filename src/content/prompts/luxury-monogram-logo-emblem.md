Monograms are a good fit for today's image models because they finally render short text reliably, and a monogram is mostly two or three letters plus a name. The prompt keeps the text short on purpose: the more words you ask for, the more chances the model has to misspell one.

Replace the bracketed placeholders before you run it. Two letters interlock best; three works if the middle one is narrow. Long names wrap or squeeze, so if yours is long, use only the surname under the mark.

PRESENTATION is the clause that makes it feel expensive. A flat logo on white looks like a template, whereas the same mark debossed and foil-stamped on black paper reads as a finished brand piece, and it hides small edge imperfections in the shading. If you need a clean file for actual printing, run a second pass with "flat black vector logo on a pure white background" and trace it in a vector tool.

The "completely original" line, together with the ban on real brand logos, keeps the model from drifting toward existing fashion-house marks.
